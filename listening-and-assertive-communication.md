# Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

Active listening means paying complete attention to the speaker and trying to understand their message, thoughts and feelings. The following strategies help us become better active listeners:

1. **Stay Focused:** Give your full attention to the speaker and avoid distractions such as mobile phones or unnecessary thoughts.
2. **Listen Carefully:** Concentrate on what the speaker is saying instead of thinking about what you are going to reply.
3. **Don't Interrupt:** Allow the speaker to complete their thoughts before expressing your opinion.
4. **Show Interest:** Use words like "Yes", "Okay" and "I understand" to encourage the speaker to continue.
5. **Use Positive Body Language:** Maintain natural eye contact, nod your head and use appropriate facial expressions to show interest.
6. **Ask Questions:** Ask relevant questions whenever something is unclear to gain a better understanding.
7. **Take Notes:** Note down important points during meetings or discussions to remember key information.
8. **Paraphrase the Message:** Explain the speaker's main points in your own words to confirm that you have understood them correctly.

By following these strategies, we can improve our listening skills and build better relationships with others.

# Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

Reflective listening is a communication technique in which we carefully listen to someone and reflect their thoughts and feelings in our own words to ensure that we have understood them correctly.

The key points of reflective listening are:

1. **Listen Carefully:** Pay attention to the speaker without interrupting or making unnecessary comments.
2. **Understand the Message:** Focus on understanding the speaker's thoughts, feelings and intentions.
3. **Remember Important Information:** Try to remember the key points shared during the conversation.
4. **Paraphrase What You Heard:** Repeat the speaker's message in your own words to confirm your understanding.
5. **Summarize the Discussion:** Briefly explain the main points to ensure that nothing important has been missed.
6. **Keep an Open Mind:** Respect different opinions and avoid judging the speaker.
7. **Show Empathy:** Try to understand the speaker's feelings and respond in a supportive manner.

These techniques help reduce misunderstandings, build trust and improve communication.

# Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

There are several obstacles that sometimes affect my listening skills:

1. I sometimes get distracted by my mobile phone or other activities around me.
2. Sometimes I start preparing my response before the other person finishes speaking.
3. When someone speaks continuously for a long time, I sometimes lose my concentration.
4. Sometimes I make assumptions without listening to the complete information.
5. When someone uses unfamiliar English words or technical terms, I may find it difficult to understand the message.
6. Sometimes my personal thoughts prevent me from giving complete attention to the speaker.

These obstacles can affect my understanding and lead to communication gaps. Identifying them is the first step towards improving my listening skills.

# Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

I can improve my listening skills by developing better listening habits and following these practices:

1. I will avoid using my mobile phone or engaging in other activities while someone is speaking.
2. I will concentrate on the speaker and give my complete attention.
3. I will allow others to finish speaking before sharing my thoughts.
4. I will ask questions whenever I have doubts instead of making assumptions.
5. I will maintain eye contact and use positive body language to show interest.
6. I will take notes during important meetings, discussions and technical sessions.
7. I will try to understand other people's opinions and feelings without judging them.
8. I will practise listening to English conversations, interviews and discussions to improve my concentration and understanding.

By practising these habits regularly, I can become a better listener and communicate more effectively.

# Question 5: When do you switch to Passive Communication Style in your day-to-day life?

I usually switch to a passive communication style when I feel uncomfortable expressing my opinions or when I interact with people who are more experienced or senior to me.

For example, during group discussions, I may have a different opinion but hesitate to express it because I am afraid of disagreements. Sometimes I agree with others even when I am not completely comfortable with their decisions.

I usually behave this way to avoid conflicts and maintain good relationships with others. However, I understand that expressing my thoughts is also important, and I should develop the confidence to share my opinions.

# Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive Communication Style in your day-to-day life?

I sometimes switch to an aggressive communication style when I become angry, frustrated or emotionally disturbed.

For example, when someone repeatedly interrupts me or ignores my opinions, I may raise my voice or respond rudely. Sometimes, when things do not happen as expected, I react immediately without thinking about my words.

I understand that aggressive communication can hurt other people's feelings and damage relationships. Therefore, I should learn to control my emotions, listen patiently and express my opinions calmly.

# Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive Communication Style in your day-to-day life?

I sometimes use a passive-aggressive communication style when I feel hurt or angry but hesitate to express my feelings directly.

For example, instead of telling someone that their behaviour has hurt me, I may remain silent, give short replies or avoid talking to them.

Sometimes I may also use sarcasm or indirect comments to show that I am upset.

I understand that this behaviour does not solve problems and may create misunderstandings. Instead, I should communicate my feelings honestly and politely so that problems can be resolved in a healthy way.

# Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

Assertive communication means expressing our thoughts, feelings, needs and opinions confidently and clearly while respecting the feelings and opinions of others.

I can develop assertive communication by following these practices:

1. **Express My Thoughts Clearly:** I will share my opinions and feelings honestly instead of hiding them or keeping them to myself.
2. **Understand My Needs:** I will identify what I need and communicate it clearly to others.
3. **Start with Small Situations:** I will practise assertive communication with my friends and family to gradually build confidence.
4. **Maintain Positive Body Language:** I will maintain eye contact, use a calm tone of voice and maintain confident body language.
5. **Speak at the Right Time:** If something bothers me, I will communicate it early instead of keeping it inside until I become frustrated.
6. **Respect Other People's Opinions:** I will listen to others and respect their viewpoints, even when they differ from mine.
7. **Learn to Say No:** I will politely refuse requests when I am uncomfortable or unable to fulfil them instead of agreeing to everything.
8. **Use Positive Language:** I will use respectful and constructive words while expressing my thoughts and feelings.
